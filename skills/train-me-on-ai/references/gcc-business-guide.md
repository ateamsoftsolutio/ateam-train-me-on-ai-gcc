# GCC business guide

Rules and official places that GCC business problems often touch: VAT, corporate tax and e-invoicing, workforce nationalisation, government guidance on AI, Arabic AI models and in-region hosting, tender portals, and the working week and Ramadan hours.

**Checked on 30 October 2026** against the sources linked. Rules, deadlines and portals change often. If this file is more than three months old, say so before using it, and always send the person to the official source.

## How to use this guide

- **This guide and its official links only.** Give each fact with its source link. If you can browse, you may open the official links here, or find the authority's own page for something not covered, and say what that page says, with the link and the date you read it. Otherwise, if something isn't here, say so and point to the authority's site. Never fill a gap from memory, and never guess a date, rate, threshold or quota.
- **Dates that have passed.** If a date in this guide is today or has passed, say so first, and send them to their accountant or the authority now.
- **Don't decide for them whether a rule applies.** Explain what the source says, including any thresholds. If they've given a figure, you can say which group it seems to point to, including when that group's date is earlier than the latest one or has passed, but their accountant, PRO or lawyer confirms, because the official texts define how figures such as revenue are counted. Never state their e-invoicing wave or phase, nationalisation band or quota as fact: that comes from the authority's own notice or portal. Help them prepare the questions to ask.
- **Sources.** Links with no label are official (a government body, regulator or official news agency, or the company that makes a product). Other links are labelled: News, Law firm, Big Four, Accounting firm, ILO, U.S. government or Intergovernmental. Say when a point rests only on one of these.
- **Pointers, not advice.** This is practical guidance, not legal or tax advice. Their accountant, PRO or lawyer confirms anything they act on.
- **Searches stay general.** Never put their figures, customers' or staff's names, or confidential details into a web search.

## 1. VAT, corporate tax and e-invoicing

An AI chat tool isn't an e-invoicing system. Where e-invoicing is mandatory, invoices must come from a system that meets the official rules below. AI helps people understand those rules, prepare questions for their accountant and e-invoicing provider, and build readiness checklists from the official pages.

### E-invoicing

**UAE** (a Ministry of Finance programme; onboarding through the FTA's EmaraTax):
- Businesses in scope must appoint an Accredited Service Provider (ASP): an e-invoicing provider accredited under the Ministry's rules. The Ministry publishes the list ([Ministry of Finance: accredited providers](https://mof.gov.ae/en/about-us/initiatives/einvoicing/einvoicing-accredited-service-providers-asps/), updated 30 October 2026).
- Revenue of AED 50 million or more: the deadline to appoint an ASP was **30 October 2026**, and the Ministry said there would be no further extension; go-live is by **1 January 2027**. Revenue below AED 50 million: appoint by **31 March 2027** and go live by **1 July 2027**. Government entities: appoint by 31 March 2027 and go live by 1 October 2027 ([Ministerial Decision 66 of 2026, amending Decision 244 (PDF)](https://mof.gov.ae/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Ministerial-Resolution-No.-66-of-2026-Amending-Certain-Provisions-of-Ministerial-Resolution-No.-244-of-2025-Regarding-the-Implementation-of-the-Electronic-Invoicing-System-En-20260514.pdf); [Ministry of Finance: e-invoicing programme update (PDF)](https://mof.gov.ae/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/UAE-eInvoicing-Programme-30June2026.pdf); [Ministerial Decision 244 of 2025 (PDF)](https://mof.gov.ae/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Ministerial-Decision-No.-244-of-2025-on-the-Implementation-of-the-Electronic-Invoicing-System.pdf)).
- Sales to consumers are outside the system until the Minister decides otherwise, and the decision defines revenue as gross income in the most recent accounting period: their accountant confirms whether and when it applies to them. Voluntary adoption has been allowed since 1 July 2026 ([Decision 244 of 2025 (PDF)](https://mof.gov.ae/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Ministerial-Decision-No.-244-of-2025-on-the-Implementation-of-the-Electronic-Invoicing-System.pdf)).
- Not covered here: what an e-invoice must contain, and whether PDF copies can still be sent alongside. Ask the provider or the accountant, or read the Ministry's own pages.

**Saudi Arabia** (ZATCA's "Fatoora"):
- Phase 1 («المرحلة الأولى» on ZATCA's Arabic page), generating and storing e-invoices electronically, has applied to all resident VAT taxpayers since 4 December 2021. Handwritten invoices and invoices made in text-editing or spreadsheet software aren't allowed ([ZATCA: e-invoicing comes into force, 4 December 2021](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/MediaCenter/News/Pages/E-invoicing-Enter-Into-Force.aspx); [ZATCA: roll-out phases](https://www.zatca.gov.sa/en/E-Invoicing/Introduction/Pages/Roll-out-phases.aspx); [the same page in Arabic](https://zatca.gov.sa/ar/E-Invoicing/Introduction/Pages/Roll-out-phases.aspx)).
- Phase 2 («المرحلة الثانية»), integration with ZATCA's Fatoora platform («الربط والتكامل» in ZATCA's Arabic news), has rolled out in waves of taxpayers since 1 January 2023. ZATCA notifies each wave at least six months ahead (same pages). In Arabic, ZATCA calls each wave «المجموعة», for example «المجموعة الخامسة والعشرين».
- **Wave 24** (announced 26 September 2025) covered taxpayers whose revenue subject to VAT exceeded SAR 375,000 in 2022, 2023 or 2024: integration by **30 June 2026**, now passed ([ZATCA: Wave 24](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/Pages/news-1426.aspx); [Big Four: KPMG Saudi Arabia](https://kpmg.com/sa/en/insights/tax-insights/tax-flash-announcement-of-24-wave-for-e-invoicing-phase-2-implementation.html)).
- **Wave 25** (announced 24 July 2026) covers taxpayers whose revenue subject to VAT exceeded SAR 187,500 in 2022, 2023, 2024 or 2025: integration by **1 February 2027** ([ZATCA: Wave 25](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/MediaCenter/News/Pages/Wave25-E-invoicing.aspx); [the same news in Arabic](https://zatca.gov.sa/ar/MediaCenter/News/Pages/Wave25-E-invoicing.aspx)).
- So, going by these descriptions, a business whose revenue subject to VAT exceeded SAR 375,000 in 2022, 2023 or 2024 seems to fall in Wave 24 or an earlier wave, and its integration deadline has most likely passed. Tell them to check their ZATCA notice today, and to ask their accountant.

**Oman** (Tax Authority). These points come from news and a Big Four firm; the decision's own text wasn't opened:
- Decision 189/2026 (Official Gazette, 9 August 2026): VAT-registered businesses must issue tax invoices electronically, in XML, through the system the Tax Authority approves; paper, PDF and emailed images won't count. From **1 April 2027** for businesses with annual supplies above OMR 5 million, and from **1 October 2027** for those below ([News: Oman Observer, 9 August 2026](https://www.omanobserver.om/article/1194184/business/oman-mandates-electronic-tax-invoices-from-april-2027)).
- The decision doesn't make clear whether government entities follow these dates, or how annual supplies are counted ([Big Four: KPMG Oman, August 2026](https://kpmg.com/om/en/insights/2026/08/oman-tax-authority-amends-vat-executive-regulations.html)).

**Bahrain:** no e-invoicing mandate or date found. PwC, writing on 27 February 2025: "the market expects that e-Invoicing, similar to the framework introduced in Saudi Arabia, is coming soon to Bahrain" ([Big Four: PwC tax summaries, Bahrain](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/bahrain/corporate/other-taxes)).

**Qatar:** the Council of Ministers approved a draft e-invoicing law on 6 May 2026. EY said on 7 May 2026 that it would next go to the Shura Council and then the Amir. EY: "Although no dates have been confirmed, e-invoicing is anticipated to be implemented from 1 January 2027." That date isn't confirmed ([Big Four: EY, 7 May 2026](https://globaltaxnews.ey.com/news/2026-1020)).

**Kuwait:** no e-invoicing mandate found ([Big Four: PwC tax summaries, Kuwait](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/kuwait)).

### UAE corporate tax

- 0% on taxable income up to AED 375,000 and 9% above it ([Cabinet Decision 116 of 2022 (PDF)](https://mof.gov.ae/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Cabinet-Decision-No-116-of-2022-on-the-annual-Taxable-Income-subject-to-Corporate-Tax.pdf)). It applies from a business's first financial year starting on or after 1 June 2023, and free zone businesses have their own rules ([Big Four: PwC tax summaries, UAE (reviewed 9 September 2026)](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/united-arab-emirates/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income)).
- Small Business Relief, for revenue of AED 3 million or less, has been extended to tax periods ending on or before 31 December 2029 ([News: Aletihad, carrying the state news agency WAM, 7 August 2026](https://en.aletihad.ae/news/uae/4683761/ministry-of-finance-extends-small-business-relief-for-corpor)). Conditions apply: their accountant confirms whether they qualify.
- Corporate tax in the other GCC countries isn't covered here: send them to their accountant.

High level only. Quotas, bands and fees change and depend on the business's size, sector and activity: never work out someone's quota. Point them to the authority's own portal and their PRO or HR adviser. Staff personal data never goes into an AI chat tool (`data-safety.md`).

### Where data can be kept in the region

"Stored in the region" isn't the same as "processed in the region". An AI system can keep its records in the UAE while the AI model reads each message somewhere else. When a business must keep data in its country or region (`privacy-laws.md`), ask where the data is stored **and** where the AI model processes it (`ai-proposal-check`, area 7).

## 6. Working week and Ramadan hours

The laws set maximum hours and a minimum weekly rest. In Saudi Arabia and Bahrain the law makes Friday the weekly rest day unless another day is agreed or set (see the table). Qatar's labour ministry has said its law makes Friday the rest day for all workers except shift workers ([News: The Peninsula, 27 March 2017](https://thepeninsulaqatar.com/article/27/03/2017/Workers-must-get-Friday-off-Ministry)). In the UAE the off-day is the one in the employment contract or work rules. For Kuwait and Oman the sources here give only the length of the weekly rest, not the day: check the contract and ask a PRO. The DIFC and ADGM free zones have their own rules (see the UAE row). Ramadan hours are announced again each year: check the current year's announcement before planning rotas.

# Curriculum

Six core sessions and seven optional modules. Every session follows the pattern in `SKILL.md`, Step 4 (recap, the idea, before any real material, watch me, your turn, check it, make it a habit, log it), and uses one of the person's real tasks.

## Placing the person

| Level | Signs | Sessions |
|---|---|---|
| **Starter** | Uses AI less than once a week, or never | All six core sessions, in order |
| **Regular** | Uses AI most weeks, mostly for writing | All six core sessions, with session 1 shortened: the request checklist, the data check, one watch me on a made-up example of their own task (skipping the side-by-side comparison), then their own request, check it, the habit and the log |
| **Confident** | Uses AI daily, and already has saved instructions or projects | A short version of session 2, then sessions 5 and 6 |

- If the signs are mixed, choose the lower level. They can move faster through anything they already do well.
- Every level gets the data check ("Before any real material") in their first session, whichever session that is.
- Add up to two optional modules. If their customers write in Arabic, recommend "Working in Arabic and English". If they manage staff, recommend "Your team's AI rules". If they've raised a question another module answers (for example e-invoicing or tenders: "Government paperwork and tenders"), offer to swap it in, or add it as a third if they'd like. Place each module after the core session it builds on, or at the end.

## The request checklist (used in every session)

A good request tells the AI what you'd tell a capable new assistant on their first day:

| Part | Ask yourself | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Context** | What's the situation, and what does it need to know about us? | "We're a building-materials trader in Dubai. This customer has bought from us for 3 years." |
| **Goal** | What should happen after this is read or used? | "I want them to accept a 2-week delay without cancelling." |
| **Audience** | Who reads it, and what do they care about? | "A procurement manager who cares about the site schedule." |
| **Format** | Length, structure, tone, language | "Under 120 words, polite and direct, in English, ending with one clear next step." |
| **Example** | Have you got one you liked? | "Here's a message we sent last year that worked well." |
| **Limits** | What must it not do or change? | "Don't promise a new date; don't offer a discount." |

Not every request needs all six. The first three do most of the work.

## Core sessions

### Session 1: Ask well, and get your first real result

- **Goal:** a result they'd actually use, on a task they do often.
- **The idea:** AI does far better when you tell it what you'd tell a new assistant.
  1. Say who it's for and what you want to happen.
  2. Give the background it can't know.
  3. Say the format and length, and show an example if you have one.
- **Before any real material:** this is where the person first meets the data rules. Explain the red list in `data-safety.md` in two sentences, help them remove names, phone numbers and IDs from their example, and check the account type of the tool it's going into. On a phone, suggest typing the example in one line (what, how many, when, where) rather than copying a message and editing it.
- **Real tasks to choose from:** a WhatsApp or email reply to a customer, in Arabic or English; a quotation cover note; a message to a supplier; a staff announcement; a follow-up after a meeting. In a clinic, a general message (opening hours, fees, how to book) or a made-up patient query, never a real one.
- **Watch me:** a weak request ("Write a reply to this customer") against a strong one built with the request checklist. Show both results, and point to the parts of the request that made the difference.
- **Your turn:** a second example of the same task, or the same one written by them.
- **Check it:** names, facts and prices right; tone right; nothing promised that they can't deliver; nothing sensitive in it.
- **Habit:** use the checklist for the next three messages of this kind.
- **Success sign:** they'd send the result with only small edits.

### Session 2: Check its work

- **Goal:** know when to trust AI, and how to check it quickly.
- **The idea:** AI can be fluent and wrong at the same time.
  1. Check every name, number, date and quotation against the source.
  2. Ask it to show where each point comes from.
  3. The more it matters, the more you check. You stay responsible for what you send or sign.
- **Before any real material:** remove names, signatures and contact details, and leave out any document whose confidentiality terms don't allow sharing it.
- **Real tasks:** a summary of a contract, supplier terms, a tender, a policy or a long email thread.
- **Watch me:** ask for a summary, then ask it to list each point with the clause or line it comes from, and to say what's missing or unclear. Spot-check two points against the source.
- **Your turn:** they write the request for another document, including "quote the clause for each point" and "what's missing or unclear?".
- **Check it:** spot-check three points; look for what matters but isn't there, such as dates, penalties, renewal terms and payment terms.
- **Habit:** never act on an AI summary of a contract or a money matter without checking the key points.
- **Say clearly:** AI helps them read faster and ask better questions. What a contract means legally is for a lawyer.

### Session 3: Work with numbers

- **Goal:** answers from a spreadsheet without writing formulas.
- **The idea:** AI can read a spreadsheet and explain it, but it must show its working.
  1. Delete the name, ID and contact columns first, and any column about a person's health, pay or performance. In a clinic, that includes diagnoses and test results: use totals or made-up rows.
  2. Ask a business question, not "analyse this".
  3. Ask for the calculation, and check one number yourself.
- **Before any real material:** go through the columns with them before anything is uploaded. Rows are fine once every column about a person is deleted; in a clinic that means name, ID, contact, diagnosis, test results and notes. Totals are safer still.
- **A sample or the whole sheet?** A few rows are enough to work out the question and the method. Totals, rankings and growth need the whole sheet with those columns deleted, or ask the AI for the formula and run it in your own spreadsheet.
- **Real tasks:** sales by product or month, expenses, overdue payments, stock levels, staff hours.
- **Tool note:** reading a file and calculating are different abilities. If their tool can read a file but can't calculate reliably, ask for the formula and have them run it in their spreadsheet. If it can't read files, paste a small table.
- **Watch me:** "Analyse this" against "Which five products grew most in the last three months compared with the three before? Show a table and how you calculated it."
- **Your turn:** their own business question on their own sheet.
- **Check it:** recalculate one figure in the spreadsheet; check that totals match the sheet and no rows were dropped.
- **Habit:** one numbers question every month, using the saved request.

### Session 4: Research and decide

- **Goal:** research a business question with real sources and turn it into a decision note.
- **The idea:** AI can search and summarise, but its sources must be real and recent.
  1. Ask for sources with links and dates, and open the important ones.
  2. Ask for options with pros, cons and costs, not a single answer.
  3. Ask what it doesn't know, and what would change the answer.
- **Before searching:** keep their customers', staff's and partners' names, their own figures and confidential details out of the search. Public names, such as a competitor or a software product, are fine.
- **Real tasks:** choosing a supplier or software, entering another GCC country or city, pricing a service, a competitor scan, a question about a GCC rule (start from the official places in `gcc-business-guide.md`, with an adviser to confirm).
- **Tool note:** this needs web search. Without it, the AI can still structure the question and the options, but every fact must be checked elsewhere. Say so.
- **Watch me:** a decision note with the question, the options, the criteria, a recommendation, what would change it, and the sources.
- **Your turn:** their own question.
- **Check it:** open two sources and check their dates and any numbers quoted from them.
- **Habit:** use it before the next purchase or decision above an amount they choose.

### Session 5: Make it repeatable

- **Goal:** stop retyping, by turning good requests into saved instructions.
- **The idea:** anything you ask more than twice should be set up once.
  1. Save your best requests as templates with blanks to fill.
  2. Write a short "about us" and "how we write" note once, and reuse it.
  3. Use your tool's saved-instructions feature if it has one, for example projects or custom assistants (the names vary by tool).
- **Before any real material:** past messages used to learn their voice have names and contact details removed.
- **Real tasks:** a company profile and voice guide; a saved setup for quotations or customer replies; a library of templates.
- **Watch me:** build a voice guide from three of their past messages.
- **Your turn:** they set up one saved instruction or project for their most common task, and test it on a new example.
- **Check it:** does it follow their voice, and are the facts in their profile right?
- **Habit:** use the saved setup for that task all week.

### Session 6: Delegate a whole job

- **Goal:** hand a recurring job to AI with the right checks, and know when a tool or a build partner is needed.
- **The idea:** a job is a trigger, steps, a check and an output.
  1. Write the job down as steps a new employee could follow.
  2. Decide where a person must check before anything goes to a customer or moves money.
  3. Use what your tool can connect to safely. If the job needs other systems, that's a no-code tool or a build partner.
- **Before connecting anything:** follow "Connecting tools" in `data-safety.md`. Only connect work email, calendars or files to a business or team account, give the least access the job needs, and never connect systems holding patient or payment data.
- **Real tasks:** a weekly sales or cash summary, sorting incoming enquiries, meeting notes into actions, a monthly comparison of supplier prices.
- **Tool note:** connections to email, calendar and files, and scheduled tasks, help here if their tool has them. Otherwise, a weekly routine with a saved request does most of the job.
- **Watch me:** write a job card: trigger, inputs, steps, checks, output, owner.
- **Your turn:** they write the job card for one of their own jobs, and set it up as far as their tool allows.
- **Check it:** run it once on safe material; a person checks before anything is sent.
- **Habit:** run it on schedule for two weeks and log the time.
- **Next:** if the job needs their customer database, accounting software or WhatsApp Business connected, it's a candidate for `ai-opportunity-map` and a build brief.

## Optional modules

| Module | What it covers | What they leave with |
|---|---|---|
| **Your team's AI rules** | Approved tools and account types, the data red list, the checking rule, who to ask, and when to tell customers AI was used. Built from `data-safety.md` and, for their countries, `privacy-laws.md`. | A one-page AI policy for staff, in their language. Recommend an adviser reviews it in regulated sectors such as healthcare and finance. |
| **Sales and customer service** | Enquiry replies, quotation drafts, follow-ups, handling objections, message templates | A set of saved templates, with a check for prices and promises |
| **Marketing and social media** | Posts, campaign ideas, Arabic and English versions, brand voice, and planning around Ramadan, Eid and national days | A content routine and a seasonal calendar. Never fake reviews or testimonials; check claims, image rights and cultural fit. In healthcare, a doctor checks every health statement before it's published. |
| **Meetings and management** | Agendas, notes into actions, updates for the board or investors, drafts of difficult messages | Templates for each, with personal details kept out |
| **Hiring and HR** | Job descriptions, interview questions, onboarding checklists | Templates without candidates' personal data. Questions about labour law go to an adviser. |
| **Working in Arabic and English** | Writing in Arabic and English for Gulf customers (formal Arabic or a Gulf tone, as suits the audience), translating with checks, bilingual documents, and drafting for staff in the other languages they use, such as Hindi, Urdu, Malayalam or Tagalog | A bilingual voice guide and checked templates. A native speaker checks important customer-facing text, and a lawyer checks the Arabic of anything contractual. |
| **Government paperwork and tenders** | Reading a tender from an official portal and planning the response; checklists for VAT, e-invoicing readiness or workforce-nationalisation reports to go through with their accountant or PRO; drafting letters to authorities. Facts come only from `gcc-business-guide.md` and its official links | A tender-reading routine and checklists. Never filing, legal or tax advice: their accountant, PRO or lawyer confirms. |

## Real tasks by industry (to suggest when they're unsure)

| Industry | Tasks that suit sessions 1 to 6 |
|---|---|
| Trading and distribution (for example in Dubai or Jeddah) | WhatsApp replies, quotation cover notes, supplier comparisons, payment reminders, stock reports |
| Retail and e-commerce | Product descriptions, promotions, customer replies, weekly sales questions |
| Clinics and healthcare | General information leaflets, staff rotas, supplier quotes, policies, monthly totals. Never patient records or real patient messages (`data-safety.md`); a doctor checks every health statement before it's used. |
| Car dealers and parts | Arabic and English enquiry replies, test-drive follow-ups, parts quotations, monthly sales questions |
| Logistics and freight | Shipment update messages, rate quotations, checking documents, customer FAQs |
| Real estate | Listing descriptions, enquiry replies, summaries of tenancy contracts |
| Hospitality and food | Bilingual menus, replies to reviews, staff rotas (including Ramadan hours), supplier price checks |
| Professional services | Proposals, tenders, research, meeting notes, client updates |
| Construction and interiors | Client updates, site reports, summaries of quantities and quotations |

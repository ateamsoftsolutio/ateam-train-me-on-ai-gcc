# Data safety

Apply this before the person pastes, uploads or connects anything real, in every skill. Explain it in plain words the first time, then keep it short. These rules are about sharing material with AI chat tools. A system properly built for personal data, with the right contracts and approvals, is a different matter for a build partner and an adviser (`privacy-laws.md`).

## Traffic lights

| | What | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Red: never** | Passwords, one-time codes and security keys; bank account and card numbers; national ID, residence, visa and passport numbers (Emirates ID, Saudi national ID or Iqama, Omani civil number, Qatar ID, Bahraini CPR, Kuwaiti civil ID); anyone's health information, including diagnoses and test results even with the name removed; people's contact details (phone numbers, email addresses, home addresses); salaries, performance or disciplinary details of named staff; customer lists with people's names or contact details; anything shared with them under a confidentiality agreement that doesn't allow it | Don't paste it into any AI chat tool. Remove it first, or use a made-up example. |
| **Amber: only with care** | Business figures (sales, margins, prices), internal documents, contracts with personal details removed, customer messages with names and contact details removed, customers' company names, businesses' tax and trade registration numbers (TRN, VAT or commercial registration numbers: swap in a made-up number of the same length where you can) | Fine on a business or team account that doesn't use their data for training, or on a personal account with training switched off. If neither, don't paste it: describe it in a line, or use a made-up version. |
| **Green: fine** | Public information, their own drafts, general questions, made-up examples | Go ahead. |

In healthcare, anything about a patient (messages, records, test results) uses made-up examples only. A real patient's message stays out even with the name removed, because the health details alone can identify someone. Business material with no patient details, such as totals, supplier quotes and rotas by role, follows the usual rules.

## Making material safe

- Replace names with roles: "Customer A", "Supplier B", "the site engineer".
- Delete name, ID, phone, email and address columns from spreadsheets before uploading, and any column about a person's health, pay or performance.
- A few rows are enough to work out a method. Totals need the whole sheet with those columns deleted, or a formula run in their own spreadsheet.
- Round or scale numbers when the exact figure doesn't matter to the question.
- Summarise instead of pasting: "a customer is upset about a two-week delay on a cement order" is enough to draft a reply.
- For documents, paste only the parts the question needs.

## Which account to check

Whatever they paste goes to the tool they paste it into. That includes the tool running this course, which may not be the one they use at work. Check each one:

- **Business and team accounts** generally don't train on customer data by default, and come with business terms.
- **Free and personal accounts** vary: some train on chats unless you switch it off, and some ask you to choose.

The vendor-by-vendor details, with sources and dates, are in "How each vendor uses your data" in `tools-and-costs.md`. Put the relevant row into plain words and help them find the setting. If their product isn't in the table, don't guess its policy: treat it as a personal account that may train on chats, and help them find its privacy settings. If you can't confirm their account type, treat it as personal.

## Connecting tools

Some AI tools can connect to email, calendars, files or other apps. Before connecting anything:

- Check the notes for their country in `privacy-laws.md`, because a mailbox or shared folder holds other people's personal data.

- Connect work accounts only to a business or team AI account, never to a free or personal one.
- Give the least access the job needs: read-only where possible, and only the folders or mailboxes involved.
- Never connect systems that hold patient records, payment details or staff files.
- A person approves anything sent to a customer, supplier or bank.
- Every few months, review what's connected and remove what isn't used.

## Documents from others

Vendor proposals, contracts and supplier documents are often marked confidential. Before they share one, check that sharing it with an AI tool is allowed, and remove any personal details it contains.

## If something sensitive was shared

Respond calmly and in proportion:

1. **Say so straight away,** in one sentence, without repeating the sensitive details.
2. **Remove it where you can.** If the tool can delete a single message, delete it. If it can only delete the whole chat: when training is already off (or it's a business account), finish the session, give them the updated AI plan and have them save a copy outside this chat (for example in a notes app), then have them delete the chat; otherwise have them copy the plan first, delete the chat now and carry on in a new one.
3. **Check the account's training setting,** and switch training off if it's on. Switching it off may not undo training already under way.
4. **Carry on with a safe version,** for example a one-line description or a made-up example.
5. **When to suggest an adviser:** if what was shared included anyone's health information, an ID, bank or card number, or more than one person's details, suggest, in one calm line, that they check with whoever advises them on data protection (often their lawyer) whether anything needs to be recorded or reported (`privacy-laws.md`). For a single contact detail deleted at once, say nothing further. Never say what the law does or doesn't require.

## Results that leave the business

- AI-written text that goes to customers, regulators or the public is the business's responsibility. A person checks it first, and in healthcare a doctor checks every health statement.
- Don't let AI invent quotes, reviews, testimonials, certifications or statistics.
- Images and text produced with AI can raise copyright questions. For anything published widely, use a business account and check the tool's terms on rights.
